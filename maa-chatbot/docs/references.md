# References — Data Sources for the MAA Knowledge Base

MAA has no real website or live data, so the knowledge base was built from two kinds
of sources:

1. **The official MAA Project Description document** (given by the instructor) used directly for anything specific to MAA itself. 

2. **General online research**, used only to add realistic depth around topics the MAA description does not go into detail on. Below are the sources actually used:

## Motherly Qualities and Supportive Communication
- The Minds Journal — "8 Qualities of a Good Mother That Guarantee A+ Parenting!"
    Used for ideas such as unconditional love and caring.

- Self Love Mom — "20 Qualities of a Good Mother: An Honest Checklist"
    Used for warmth, consistency and responsive caregiving.

- Mom Therapy Chicago (Amy Braun LCPC) — "20 Motherly Characteristics"
    Used for information about patience and other motherly qualities.

- Medium — Daimond Simon — "7 Signs of a Good Mother, According to Psychology"
    Used for empathy, compassion and emotional intelligence.

- Sweetymom — "What are the Qualities of a Good Mother?"
    Used for consistency, accountability and self-care.

- Medium — Muhammad Tahir Rashid — "Unconditional Love: The Timeless Power of a Mother's Devotion"
    Used for ideas related to a mother's care and constant support.

- Communication Theory — "Active Listening Skills — Techniques And Tips To Practice It"
    Used for active listening and responding to someone's feelings before giving advice.

- LinkedIn Collaborative Article — "How do you use active listening and empathy to validate someone's feelings?"
    Used for understanding and validating emotions.

- Grand Rising Behavioral Health — "The Importance of Active Listening in Supporting Mental Health"
    Used for the connection between active listening and emotional support.

- Heartfelt Texts — "25 Heartfelt Words to Comfort a Homesick Loved One"
    Used for the idea that homesickness is a normal feeling.

- NPR (The Salt), Saveur and Substack discussions
    Used for the idea of food as a way of showing love and care, especially in South Asian families.

## General Health Information

The following sources were used for some of the additional health-related information in medicine.json:

- NCBI PMC — Research on reminders and their effect on following health routines.
- NCBI PMC — Research related to SMS reminders and medication adherence.
- Science of Essentials — "25 Simple Health Tips to Improve Your Daily Life" for general information about hydration, mood, energy and sleep habits.
- Mutual of Omaha — "Tips for Including Healthy Habits into Your Daily Routine" for general healthy routines.

## Travel Safety

Some additional travel information in travel.json was based on the following sources:

- Penn State Education Abroad — "Travel Safety Tips" for researching destinations and emergency numbers before travelling.
- The Wrong Way Home — "Tips On Staying Safe in Unfamiliar Cities" for staying alert and protecting belongings.
- Accor — "Travel Safety Tips: Staying Secure Abroad" for general travel safety.
- Centrum Air — "Personal Safety Tips in an Unfamiliar City" for using licensed taxis and verified ride services.
- University of Texas at Dallas — "General Safety and Security Tips for Travelers" for sharing travel plans and staying reachable.

## Pakistani Home-Cooked Food

The extra food examples in food.json were based on:

- Remitly — "Pakistani Cuisine: Rich Curries and Traditional Recipes" for common Pakistani dishes.
- Very Hungry Nomads — "15 Most Popular Foods From Pakistan" for examples of popular Pakistani food.
- Domestic Fits — "Pakistani Food: 34 Popular Dishes + 6 Secret Recipe Tips" for dishes such as shorba.

## How These Sources Were Used

The sources above were used to understand general ideas and then write the knowledge-base entries in our own words. No sentences were copied directly from these sources.

These references were only used for general topics such as communication, health habits, travel safety and food. They were not used to create MAA-specific information such as actual prices, phone numbers or policies.

MAA-specific information comes from the official MAA Project Description provided for the project. If something is not defined there, such as a paid membership plan, the chatbot does not make up the information.
